
### §16 — the parser's real coverage, written down  (2026-08-17, after the first real PDF)

Four fixes off the first exported PDF. The parsing ones matter most: each was a case that
produced a *plausible-looking* page with something quietly missing.

**1. Stay is read from prose, not just from a `Stay:` line.** Only Day 2 of a fifteen-day
programme had a Stay chip, because `STAY_RE` anchors at line start and these itineraries write
it mid-sentence: "Check-in and relax after your journey **and overnight in Cairo**", "Dinner on
board **and Overnight on the West Bank**". `OVERNIGHT_IN_TEXT` now scans each day's bullets when
no explicit line was found, taking the **last** mention — a day that moves on ends where it ends.
The bullet keeps its text. All 15 days now carry a chip except Day 15, which is a departure and
correctly has none.

`tidyStay()` normalises the capture: `on board` / `aboard` / `on the dahabiya` all become
**On board** (the old code returned the literal word "board"), and a leading preposition or
"the" is dropped, so `Overnight beside a peaceful Nile island` reads *a peaceful Nile island*.

**2. Both list headings were under-matched, and one was dangerously over-matched.** `INC_RE`'s
first alternative had no end anchor, so *"Inclusions are subject to change without notice"* read
as a heading and swallowed the rest of the document into the list. Both are now anchored at both
ends and built from a shared lead/tail, and all of these are recognised:

> INCLUDED SERVICES · Inclusions · INCLUSIONS: · Included · Included items · PACKAGE INCLUDES ·
> THE PRICE INCLUDES: · What's included · WHAT IS INCLUDED · Services included ·
> INCLUDED IN THE PRICE · Cost includes: · Tour Includes
>
> NOT INCLUDED · Exclusions · EXCLUSIONS: · Excluded · Excluded items · PACKAGE EXCLUDES ·
> THE PRICE DOES NOT INCLUDE · What is not included · NOT INCLUDED IN THE PRICE · Excluding ·
> Price excludes:

**EXC is tested before INC** — "NOT INCLUDED" contains "included".

**3. Offered packages no longer need the word OFFERED.** `offerHeading()` replaces the old
regex and requires a structural word (`offered/package/programme/option/tier/category/rates`)
**and** either a tier or a trailing colon, on a line under 72 characters. Both halves are
load-bearing: a tier alone would make *"Enjoy a luxury dinner"* and *"Wadi Rum 4x4 Jeep Safari
and luxury desert camp"* open a price table and eat the itinerary. Recognised:

> OFFERED 4 Star package : · 4 Star package: · 5★ PACKAGE · 5* package: ·
> Option 2 — Deluxe package · OFFERED PACKAGE: · Package rates — Superior · FIVE STAR PACKAGE ·
> 3 Star Package · Standard package: · OFFERED Deluxe programme: · Four Star Option:

The tier becomes the category, title-cased — "4 Star", "Five Star", "Deluxe" — not "Offered 4
Star Package". `TIER_RE` hand-rolls its boundaries because `\b` after `★` or `*` never matches
(neither is a word character), which had silently lost every `5★` heading; the trailing
lookahead still stops `4x4` reading as a tier. A tier quoting two occupancies keeps both in the
one rate cell (`8,800 USD · 11,500 USD`) instead of losing everything after the first, or filing
it under hotels.

Regression-tested in node: 24 heading variants all parse, and 8 prose lines that must NOT be
headings produce zero false positives.

**4. Cosmetic, from the PDF:** the photo stack is `justify-content: center` — four photos rarely
fill the column and the leftover gap always landed at the bottom, which read as a mistake. And
the builder has a **File name** field: the download was named from the title
(`Itinerary_1.pdf`). Spaces and `&` are kept — "Pam & Steve - Egypt & Jordan.pdf" is what belongs
in a client's inbox — and only characters a filesystem actually rejects are replaced. Blank falls
back to the title; `data.filename` stores it.

**Known limits of the parser, deliberately not guessed at:** a rate with no recognisable figure
is printed verbatim rather than blanked; an unrecognised meal phrase ("No meals", "picnic on the
reef") prints as written rather than being dropped; and a section heading is only honoured after
the first day heading, so the word "Included" inside a day's prose cannot hijack the document.
All three are the same principle — visible and wrong beats silently missing.
